Underrepresentation

Mark Dean, as an African American engineer, is one of many underrepresented inventors in the world of technology. The personal computing industry in the late 1970s was characterized by a lack of racial diversity, and few black engineers were in senior management positions in major technology companies when Dean began his career at IBM. Nevertheless, Dean became one of the chief engineers working on the IBM Personal Computer and was the first African American named as an IBM Fellow. Dean's work also brought attention to the challenges faced by underrepresented groups in the field of technology and the importance of diversity in the development process. His legacy continues to inspire future generations of engineers from diverse backgrounds and to highlight the need for greater diversity and inclusion in STEM fields.



Short Biography

Mark Dean is a computer scientist and engineer, and he worked for IBM and created many different technologies for IBM, such as the color monitor for PC and the gigahertz chip. Additionally, we own 3 of the 9 original patents that are a part of IBM. Mark Dean was born on March 2nd, 1957, in Jefferson City, Tennessee, to James and Barbara Dean. In his early life, it was very evident that he had a brain for problem-solving and building things. Actually, when he was young, he built a tractor with his father and was gifted in many different things. He was an athlete in high school as well as graduating with straight A's before going to college. He attended the University of Tennessee, where he majored in Engineering and graduated at the top of his class in 1979, and almost immediately started working for IBM. In his time at IBM, he did a lot of great things, but the first thing that he is known for is designing the Industry Standard Architecture (ISA) system bus with one of his colleagues, Dennis Moeller. This system made it possible for peripheral devices to be plugged into computers, and it allowed for faster speeds and better performance. Additionally, he was able to develop the color monitor for personal computers in the 90's, and in 1999, he and a group of engineers developed the gigahertz chip. Now Dean has returned to the University of Tennessee as a professor and is working to teach a new generation of engineers.
